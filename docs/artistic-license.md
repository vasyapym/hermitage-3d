# Artistic license register

A stylized recreation, not a survey. Every deliberate deviation from the real palace lands here, one line each, when it is introduced.

- **Route compression:** the visitor route (Palace Square → portal → compressed Rastrelli Gallery → Jordan Staircase → dark airlock → Military Gallery) is a stage designer's compression; in reality the Jordan Staircase is reached from the Neva embankment and the Palace Square arches lead to the courtyard.
- **Statue archetypes:** roof figures are abstracted silhouettes, no faces; the Jordan Staircase portal's Mars & Apollo statue niches keep their place but hold stylized vases (charter bans figures — design-staircase §2).
- **Portrait atlas (v1 stand-ins — DEC-030; design-gallery §3.4–3.6):** CLOSED (superseded by atlas-v2). v1 shipped a 2048² procedural canvas atlas painting all 332 standard canvases as stand-ins (21×17, cell-index keyed, 418,982 B) while the licensing question for real public-domain reproductions was open. The question is resolved in the atlas-v2-license row; the composition law is carried forward unchanged in structure (grid, key law, super-blocks, spare/missing cells, gutter paint, hash law, PNG encoder) at 1.5× scale. Pins moved: constants, mock RGBA/PNG digests, ATLAS_BYTES_MAX.
- **Portrait atlas hanging order (atlas-v2-hanging-order):** the content of the 335 frames follows the ru.wiki «Военная галерея» hanging schemes, transcribed to `tools/gallery/hang.source.json` (342 wall slots: 329 painted + 13 silk — both published schemes combined; the draft's 345 assumption is corrected in the transcription's own note) and reduced to the kit's 330 wall frames by the row-tail-painted rule (southmost painted surplus per wall-row dropped, silk never dropped, the drops travel up one row to fill a short row); the 12 omitted sitters are listed in `tools/gallery/roster.json`. Anchors: Alexander I (Krüger) at the portal; Franz I (Krafft) west ally; Friedrich Wilhelm III (Krüger) east ally; Kutuzov/Barclay de Tolly (Dawe) at the north door pair. The two published schemes disagree on the door pair (they split Kutuzov and Barclay across the two ends); this register rules Kutuzov/Barclay — the pair the Hermitage texts describe at the St George's exit — and records the alternative (Konstantin Pavlovich / Wellington, ГЭ-7807/7810) as a two-key edit. Positions are approximate by construction (330 kit frames vs 342 transcribed slots; the real rows' column gaps close up).
- **Portrait atlas license (atlas-v2-license):** posture A — Wikimedia Commons files of the paintings, each carrying a PD-Art-family template (allowlist in `atlas-law.mjs`), pinned by sha256 in `atlas.lock.json`. Basis: the paintings are public domain worldwide (Dawe d. 1829, Polyakov 1835, Golicke 1848, Krafft 1856, Krüger 1857); faithful reproductions of 2D PD works carry no copyright in the US, UK, DE, SE and under DSM art. 14. Russia: no explicit norm; Supreme Court practice leans the same way; the operative Russian constraint is museum law (ФЗ-54 ст. 36) on COMMERCIAL use — this project is non-commercial (owner confirmation #2). Residual risk: low, not zero; mitigation: the lockfile makes any file replaceable in one re-lock. Not legal advice.
- **Charter distinction, painted likenesses (atlas-v2-charter-distinction):** the charter non-goal "no figures" means no modelled or animated human figures; painted two-dimensional likenesses on canvases are picture content, not figures. The charter non-goal "no photographic textures of the real interiors" means no photographs of the room's surfaces (walls, floor, ceiling, frames, fittings); reproductions of the paintings that hang in the room are artworks, not interior photographs, and are the sanctioned risk-#4 material. Gilt frames, walls, floor and ceiling remain fully procedural.
- **Shared ornament vocabulary:** one gilt-ornament kit stands in for the palace's real variety.
- **Mirrored symmetry** where Rastrelli was symmetrical anyway.
- **Dimension approximations:** footprint ~210 × 175 m, principal facade ~215 m (carried in the spec as the crease line), 22 m to the cornice — sourced in `ATTRIBUTIONS.md`; the roof band (~24 m total) and courtyard proportions are declared targets, checked against sources during Phase 1 and corrected here if the record differs. Hunt verdict (literature search closed 2026-10-17): the 215 m figure is attested (wiki-en "principal facade 215 m × 30 m", citing Budberg, *Great Palaces: The Winter Palace*, Hamlyn 1969, pp. 194–201), while the ru tradition reads the same edge as 210 m — the two figures most likely measure one street edge under different source conventions rather than "plan line" vs "crease line", so the solver's 234-vs-215 crease warning marks a source-convention gap, not a settled dimensional error. Settling it needs a measured drawing (РГИА ф. 485, an Albertina scan, or Pilyavsky, *Зимний дворец*, Л. 1960, via НЭБ or paper) — none of which is digitized.
- **Facade axis counts (Phase 1 spec):** per-section window-axis counts in `src/content/facade-spec.ts` (south 7 + 20 + 8 + 20 + 7, bay pitch ~3.4 m) are photo-derived working targets, ±1 axis per section; the section structure (three risalits, triple-arch central gateway, paired columns, corner bunches, 3 window-width modules) is sourced. Hunt verdict (literature search closed 2026-10-17): the specific 7+20+8+20+7 rhythm (62 axes) and the 234 m crease length the solver derives from it are project-declared — no digitized attestation found; what the sources attest is a south facade of seven divisions with a three-arch central risalit. Photo-empirics on Commons panoramas (median window spacing ≈ 4.6–4.75 m over ~210 m) suggest materially fewer physical axes (~45–50), so the declared rhythm is a working target, not a reproduction. Counts to be checked against the measured elevation drawings (РГИА ф. 485 / Albertina / Pilyavsky 1960, 1974, 1989 — not digitized) and corrected here and in the spec if the record differs.
- **Window/decor catalogue:** the 12 window types, 22 surrounds, 32 decor types, 5 capital varieties are catalogued but modelled as a smaller stylized set (one capital archetype, a few surround families) — the grammar reproduces the module system and rhythm, not the one-for-one catalogue.

- **The Palace Square paving (DEC-041):** the square's ground reads the wiki-sourced class — the 1977 reconstruction re-laid the asphalt interlude (1932–1977) with diabase sett paving («асфальтовое покрытие заменено диабазовой брусчаткой»); every MODULE is declared: sett 0.12 m with 12 mm joints, per-sett tone ±18 %, the 3.5 m wear mottle, the light-granite band grid (period 20 m, width 0.6 m, the Column axis at a cell centre) — the band grid is a declared reading of aerial photographs, not a measured layout. An intentional hybrid: the 1977 surface reads beside the pre-lantern enclosure (DEC-037/038) — the square's signature sett ground over the era's own fence, with the asphalt interlude elided.
- **The square's footprint and the surround (DEC-041):** the paved footprint x ±110 / z 92–330 (220 × 238 m ≈ 5.2 ha against the sourced ≈ 5.4 ha), edges declared from the sourced area; beyond the footprint the plane carries a flat darker roadway tone standing in for the unmodelled flanking buildings (the Guards Corps HQ east — nothing wall-like exists in the tree; the class stayed a successor at the ground round, and the flanking architecture is its own successor).
